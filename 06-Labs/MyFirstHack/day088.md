# Day 88 — Tools You'll See
**Day 88 of 90 · Becoming Security+ Ready**  
**Date:** November 2, 2026

## Lesson Overview
Today's lesson introduces the categories of tools security professionals use. Its goal is to make security work environments familiar when job descriptions mention tools.

> **Tools are interchangeable; the thinking is what transfers.**

Products change, get renamed, or are replaced. The category and underlying job endure. Understanding logs, endpoint behaviour, network activity, vulnerabilities, and risk makes learning a specific product easier.

The toolbox supports four activities:
1. **Detect** what is happening.
2. **Prevent** what can be stopped.
3. **Investigate** what needs deeper analysis.
4. **Manage** security over time.

## 1. Why Tool Categories Matter
Job descriptions often name products, but employers may use different tools for similar work. “Or equivalent” reflects that category-level understanding and security thinking matter more than experience with one product.

Examples:
- SIEM products centralise logs, support searching, and generate alerts.
- EDR products monitor endpoint activity and support response.
- Vulnerability scanners identify systems affected by known vulnerabilities.

Focus on what a tool category does, what evidence it provides, and how to interpret that evidence—not memorising every product.

## 2. Detection Tools — Seeing What's Happening

### SIEM — Security Information and Event Management
A **SIEM** is a central tool category in a SOC. It:
- Collects logs across an organisation.
- Centralises logs from servers, workstations, network devices, applications, and other sources.
- Supports searching and correlating events.
- Generates alerts when patterns matter.

Course connection: Day 59 (logs) and Day 84 (the haystack-of-logs problem and AI assistance).

When a SOC analyst monitors alerts and investigates events, the SIEM is often their main workbench.

### EDR — Endpoint Detection and Response
**EDR** focuses on individual computers and devices, called **endpoints** (laptops, servers, workstations). It monitors:
- Processes (Day 57).
- Network connections (Day 58).
- Persistence mechanisms (Day 62).
- Suspicious behaviour.

EDR can support remote response actions such as isolating a machine, stopping a process, or gathering forensic data.

| SIEM | EDR |
|---|---|
| Centralises logs/events across an organisation. | Deep visibility into individual endpoints. |
| Search, correlation, alerting. | Endpoint monitoring and response actions. |
| Broad view across many sources. | Detailed view of a device. |

The lesson treats SIEM and EDR as two especially important detection categories used together by SOC analysts and incident responders.

### IDS/IPS — Intrusion Detection and Prevention
IDS/IPS tools monitor network traffic for signs of attack.
- **IDS:** detects and flags suspicious traffic.
- **IPS:** can also block suspicious traffic.

The lesson describes these as network-level controls, often at network boundaries.

### Network Monitoring
Network monitoring tracks traffic, flows, and anomalies. It can help catch repeated communication patterns such as beaconing (Day 58) at scale.

### AI Assistance
AI assistance (Day 84) increasingly appears inside modern SIEM and EDR products. The lesson presents it as a capability within these tools, not necessarily a separate category.

**Detection takeaway:** SOC analysts commonly use SIEM for centralised logs and alerts, EDR for endpoint visibility and response, and network monitoring/IDS feeds for network-level signals.

## 3. Prevention Tools — Enforcing Defences

### Firewalls
A firewall (Day 33) controls which network traffic is allowed. Organisations use firewalls at network boundaries and to segment internal networks.

### Secure Email Gateways
These filter incoming email for phishing and malicious attachments. Course connection: Day 15.

### IAM — Identity and Access Management
**IAM** tools control who can access which systems and resources, including authentication and access controls. The lesson notes that multi-factor authentication (MFA) is a baseline expectation.

### Endpoint Protection
Endpoint protection includes traditional antivirus and increasingly overlaps with EDR. Its prevention role is to try to block malicious software from running.

**Prevention takeaway:** Firewalls control traffic, email gateways filter malicious email, IAM controls access, and endpoint protection attempts to block malware.

## 4. Investigation Tools — Understanding What Happened

### Packet Analysers
Packet analysers inspect network traffic in detail. **Wireshark** is the lesson's example (Day 35).

### Forensic Tools
Forensic tools help investigators examine compromised systems carefully while preserving evidence. Course connection: Linux forensics capstone, Day 65.

### Sandboxes
A sandbox is an isolated environment where suspicious files or programs can be executed to observe behaviour safely. It supports malware analysis (Day 76) without running samples directly in a system that matters.

### Threat Intelligence Platforms
These aggregate information about current attacker techniques, campaigns, and indicators. The lesson connects their practical use to ATT&CK (Day 72).

**Investigation takeaway:** Packet analysers examine traffic, forensic tools examine systems while preserving evidence, sandboxes observe suspicious programs in isolation, and threat intelligence platforms provide attacker context.

## 5. Management Tools — Maintaining Security

### Vulnerability Scanners
Vulnerability scanners (Day 78) check systems against known vulnerabilities and report which CVEs affect which systems. The lesson connects this to the **Discover** step of vulnerability management.

### Patch Management Tools
These coordinate patch deployment across many systems. Course connection: Day 79.

### GRC Platforms
GRC platforms support Governance, Risk, and Compliance work:
- Document controls.
- Manage risk.
- Track compliance.
- Prepare audits.
- Map requirements to evidence.

Course connections: Days 85–86.

**Management takeaway:** Scanners identify affected systems, patch management coordinates updates, and GRC platforms support risk and compliance.

## 7. Tools and a SOC Analyst's Working Day
The lesson's SOC picture:
1. **SIEM:** central place for logs, searches, correlations, and alerts.
2. **EDR:** endpoint visibility and response.
3. **Network monitoring and IDS/IPS:** network-level signals.
4. **Investigation tools:** packet analysis, forensics, sandboxing, and threat intelligence.
5. **Management tools:** vulnerability scanning, patching, and GRC.

Tools support the work; they do not replace analyst judgement. Analysts still decide what activity means, whether an alert matters, what evidence supports a conclusion, and what response is appropriate.

## 8. Tools Change; the Thinking Endures

### What changes
- Products are acquired.
- Names change.
- Tools are replaced.
- Organisations select different products.

### What transfers
- Understanding logs and what to look for.
- Understanding endpoint behaviour.
- Understanding network traffic.
- Recognising vulnerabilities and their significance.
- Applying risk thinking.
- Using frameworks to interpret activity.

| Framework/concept | How it helps across tools |
|---|---|
| Kill chain (Day 71) | Understand attacks regardless of which tool surfaces them. |
| ATT&CK (Day 72) | Recognise and communicate attacker techniques. |
| Risk thinking (Days 67, 80, 86) | Prioritise and decide what to do with findings. |
| CIA triad | Conceptual lens for security decisions. |
| Defence in depth | Understand layered security beyond one product. |
| Threat modelling | Reason about threats and protections independent of product choice. |

An entry-level employer may not expect a candidate to have used its exact SIEM. The lesson says employers expect understanding of what a SIEM does, why it is used, and the underlying work—and can train the candidate on the specific product.

The interview message is not simply “I used Product X.” It is: **I understand what this category of tool does, why it is used, and the security thinking behind it.**

## 11. Review Questions and Answers

**Q1. What is the main principle of the lesson?**  
Tools are interchangeable; the thinking is what transfers.

**Q2. Why focus on categories instead of memorising products?**  
Products change and organisations use different ones, while the category and underlying work remain similar.

**Q3. What does SIEM do?**  
Collects and centralises logs and supports searching, correlation, and alerting.

**Q4. What is the difference between SIEM and EDR?**  
SIEM provides centralised visibility across sources; EDR monitors and supports response on individual endpoints.

**Q5. What does EDR monitor?**  
Endpoint processes, network connections, persistence mechanisms, and suspicious behaviour.

**Q6. What is the difference between IDS and IPS?**  
IDS detects and flags suspicious traffic; IPS can also block it.

**Q7. What does network monitoring help identify?**  
Traffic patterns, flows, anomalies, and beaconing at scale.

**Q8. Name four prevention tool categories.**  
Firewalls, secure email gateways, IAM tools, and endpoint protection.

**Q9. What does IAM control?**  
Who can access which systems and resources, including authentication and access controls.

**Q10. What is a sandbox used for?**  
Executing suspicious files/programs in isolation to observe their behaviour safely.

**Q11. What do forensic tools support?**  
Careful examination of compromised systems while preserving evidence.

**Q12. What do vulnerability scanners report?**  
Known vulnerabilities, including CVEs, affecting particular systems.

**Q13. What do patch management tools do?**  
Coordinate patch deployment across systems.

**Q14. What do GRC platforms support?**  
Risk and compliance management, controls documentation, audits, and evidence mapping.

**Q15. What should a beginner say if they have not used an employer's exact SIEM?**  
Explain what a SIEM does, why it is used, and the underlying log-analysis/security thinking; specific product training can follow.

## 12. Personal Learning Reflection

This lesson helped me understand the tools used in cybersecurity roles and how they fit into security-team work. Tools are grouped by purpose: detection, prevention, investigation, and management.

SIEM centralises logs and alerts. EDR focuses on endpoint activity and response. IDS/IPS and network monitoring provide network-level visibility. Packet analysers, forensic tools, sandboxes, and threat intelligence platforms support deeper investigations. Vulnerability scanners, patch management, and GRC platforms help maintain security over time.

The most important lesson is that I do not need to memorise every product or have used every tool before applying for an entry-level role. I need to understand the category, purpose, evidence it provides, and the security thinking behind it. My focus should be strengthening those fundamentals and practising with tools that demonstrate them.

## 13. Final Takeaway

The security toolbox includes **detection, prevention, investigation, and management**. SIEM and EDR are central detection categories; firewalls, email gateways, IAM, and endpoint protection enforce prevention; packet analysers, forensic tools, sandboxes, and threat intelligence support investigation; vulnerability scanners, patch management, and GRC platforms support ongoing security management.

**Tools change, but the underlying security thinking transfers.** Understanding logs, endpoints, networks, vulnerabilities, risk, and frameworks makes it easier to learn the specific products an organisation uses. For an entry-level candidate, understanding what a tool category does—and why it matters—is more important than claiming experience with every product name.
