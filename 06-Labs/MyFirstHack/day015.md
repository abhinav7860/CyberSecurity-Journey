# Day 15 - Phishing Email Analysis (Preparation)

**Platform:** MyFirstHack  
**Module:** Understanding the Digital World  
**Day:** 15 / 90

---

## Objective

Today's goal was to learn how a SOC (Security Operations Center) analyst investigates a suspicious email without interacting with any potentially malicious content.

Instead of immediately deciding whether an email is phishing, I learned how to gather evidence using industry-standard tools before making a conclusion.

---

# What I Learned

Today I learned that phishing analysis is more than just looking at the email itself.

A SOC analyst investigates multiple parts of the email, including:

- Email headers
- Sender information
- Authentication records
- Embedded URLs
- Website behaviour
- Social engineering techniques

The decision is always based on evidence rather than assumptions.

---

# Safety Rules Followed

Before starting the analysis, I followed the basic rules used by SOC analysts.

- Never clicked the link directly.
- Never downloaded any attachments.
- Used online sandbox services instead of visiting websites manually.
- Investigated everything safely before interacting with it.

---

# Email Selected

I selected an email from my Gmail Spam folder.

Collected the following information:

- Sender name
- Sender email address
- Subject
- Date received
- Purpose of the email

---

# Email Header Investigation

I opened the original email source using Gmail's **Show Original** option.

From the headers I checked:

- Sender email
- SPF
- DKIM
- DMARC
- Delivery information
- Server details

This helped me understand how email authentication works.

---

# URL Investigation

Instead of opening the URL, I copied it safely.

The URL was submitted to:

- VirusTotal
- URLScan.io

These services allowed me to inspect the website without risking my own machine.

---

# VirusTotal Analysis

I uploaded the URL to VirusTotal.

Things I observed:

- Detection ratio
- Reputation
- Redirect information
- Security vendor verdicts

This helps determine whether the URL has been reported as malicious.

---

# URLScan.io Analysis

Next, I analyzed the same URL using URLScan.io.

Things I observed:

- Screenshot of the webpage
- Redirect chain
- IP address
- Hosting provider
- Domain information
- Resources loaded by the webpage

URLScan allowed me to safely observe what would happen if a user clicked the link.

---

# Email Authentication

While reading the email headers, I learned about three important authentication mechanisms.

### SPF

Verifies whether the sending mail server is authorized to send emails for that domain.

---

### DKIM

Uses a digital signature to verify that the email was not modified during delivery.

---

### DMARC

Works with SPF and DKIM to help prevent email spoofing.
