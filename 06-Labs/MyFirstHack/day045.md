## Maya's Network

Maya's business has grown, but her network has grown without much security planning.

Everything is currently on **one flat network with no segmentation**.

The network contains:

- Two payment tills
- Four security cameras
- A back-office laptop containing business and customer information
- Maya's personal laptop
- Two staff phones
- A smart speaker
- A network printer
- Customer Wi-Fi

The biggest problem is that the **customer Wi-Fi is on the same network as the payment tills and business systems**.

### Other Problems

- Wi-Fi uses WPA2 with a password unchanged for three years
- The Wi-Fi password is written on a chalkboard
- Router administrator password is still the default
- Security cameras have never been updated
- No proper monitoring
- No logs being reviewed
- Uncertainty about the firewall
- Staff use whatever device is nearby, including the laptop containing customer data

So there are several weaknesses that connect directly to what I studied earlier.

## Assessment Framework

I learned that a network assessment should follow a proper process instead of just listing random problems.

### 1. Inventory

First, identify everything that exists on the network.

You cannot properly defend devices that you don't know about.

### 2. Identify Risks

After creating the inventory, look at each device and identify what could go wrong.

### 3. Prioritise the Risks

Not every problem has the same level of danger.

For Maya, the payment system being accessible from customer Wi-Fi is a **critical risk** because it could expose payment systems and business data.

The smart speaker is much less important.

### 4. Recommend Specific Fixes

The recommendations should be practical.

Instead of saying:

> Improve network security.

A better recommendation is:

> Put the payment tills on their own network segment and isolate them from customer Wi-Fi and IoT devices.

### 5. Consider Maya's Limitations

Maya is running a small business, so an enterprise-level solution with a large security team and expensive infrastructure would not be realistic.

The recommendations should be affordable and practical for a small business.

## Thinking Like an Attacker

I also learned to look at the network from an attacker's point of view.

If an attacker is near the shop, they could potentially target the Wi-Fi.

The password is weak from a security perspective because it has been reused for years and is publicly written inside the shop.

Once someone gets onto the Wi-Fi, the **flat network** becomes a major problem.

Because there is no segmentation, a compromised device could potentially communicate with:

- Payment tills
- Cameras
- Business laptop
- Printer
- Other devices

The default router credentials are another serious weakness.

The lack of monitoring makes things even worse because an attack may happen without anyone noticing.

The important thing I learned here is that the most dangerous weakness is the one that gives an attacker the most access for the least effort.

---

## Thinking Like a Defender

After looking at the network like an attacker, I looked at how to reduce the damage.

The biggest priority is **segmentation**.

The payment systems should be separated from:

- Customer Wi-Fi
- IoT devices
- Staff devices

Other important fixes include:

- Use strong and unique Wi-Fi passwords
- Change the router's default administrator password
- Use proper access controls
- Apply least privilege
- Update the security cameras
- Enable and properly configure the firewall
- Add monitoring and logging
- Separate customer and business networks

The goal isn't to make the network impossible to attack.

The goal is to make a compromise much harder to spread and reduce the damage if something does get compromised.

This connects directly to the **Zero Trust** lesson from Day 40: assume breach and limit the blast radius.

## Main Risks I Found

| Risk | Priority | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Flat network | Critical | A compromise could spread across the network |
| Customer Wi-Fi shares payment network | Critical | Untrusted devices can reach sensitive systems |
| Default router password | Critical | Router administration could be compromised |
| Old Wi-Fi password | High | Makes unauthorised access easier |
| Cameras never updated | High | Outdated IoT devices can become an entry point |
| No monitoring/logging | High | Attacks may go unnoticed |
| Dual-use personal laptop | Medium/High | Business data can be exposed through risky browsing |
| Staff using shared devices | Medium | Increases the chance of business data exposure |
| Smart speaker | Low | Less critical than payment and business systems |

---

## Recommended Network Structure

The network should no longer be completely flat.

A better setup would separate the different types of devices.

### Payment Network

Only the payment tills should be here.

It should be isolated from customer and IoT networks.

### Business Network

Used for Maya's business systems and authorised staff devices.

### IoT Network

Security cameras and the smart speaker should be isolated here.

### Guest Network

Customers should only have access to the internet.

They should not be able to access the payment systems, business laptop, cameras, or other internal devices.
