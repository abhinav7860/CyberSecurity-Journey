# PORTFOLIO OVERVIEW

## From Understanding Security to Thinking Like an Analyst

Over 90 days, I developed a structured foundation in cybersecurity, progressing from personal digital security and networking fundamentals to Linux investigations, attack methodologies, defensive controls, and organisational risk management.

The journey covered essential security concepts, common threats and vulnerabilities, the Cyber Kill Chain, MITRE ATT&CK, incident response, vulnerability management, and the responsibilities of cybersecurity professionals.

Through nine foundational artifacts, I explored phishing analysis, threat modelling, network traffic investigation, security architecture, Linux investigation, forensic workflows, breach analysis, and vulnerability prioritisation.

What connects these artifacts is a developing analytical approach: **identify the problem, examine available evidence, understand the potential impact, evaluate defensive options, and communicate a justified conclusion.**

Rather than treating cybersecurity as a collection of tools or definitions, this portfolio reflects my progression toward evidence-based investigation, structured problem-solving, and practical security decision-making.

This foundation supports my continued development in Security Operations, threat detection, incident investigation, and defensive cybersecurity.

# THE TEN ARTIFACTS

## 01. Personal Security Audit — Day 3

An assessment of everyday digital security practices and potential personal exposures, demonstrating an understanding of foundational security principles and practical risk reduction.

## 02. Phishing Email Analysis — Day 15

An examination of a suspicious email, focusing on sender information, message content, links, attachments, and possible deception indicators, demonstrating structured email threat analysis.

## 03. Personal Threat Model — Day 20

A structured look at personal assets, potential threat actors, vulnerabilities, attack scenarios, and associated risks, demonstrating threat-modelling fundamentals and security-oriented thinking.

## 04. Wireshark Traffic Investigation Report — Day 35

A network traffic investigation exercise using Wireshark's protocol analysis, conversations, endpoints, filtering, and packet inspection workflows, demonstrating an evidence-led approach to interpreting network activity. The final report should contain findings verified against the original capture.

## 05. Maya Network Defence Plan — Day 45

An assessment of a small-business network scenario involving flat network architecture, customer Wi-Fi exposure, router security, IoT devices, and limited monitoring. It demonstrates the ability to identify weaknesses, prioritise risks, and recommend practical defensive controls.

## 06. Linux Investigation Report — Day 56

A Linux-focused investigation exercise examining a suspicious file and its security implications, demonstrating command-line investigation, structured evidence gathering, and careful interpretation.

## 07. Linux Forensics Investigation — Day 65

A whole-system forensic investigation workflow covering processes, network connections, logs, accounts, scheduled tasks, suspicious files, evidence correlation, and timeline construction. It demonstrates a systematic approach to host investigation while distinguishing observed evidence from assumptions.

## 08. Kill Chain Breach Analysis — Day 73

An analysis of a realistic breach scenario mapped across the seven Cyber Kill Chain stages, connecting attacker actions with relevant security concepts and potential defensive interruption points.

## 09. Vulnerability Triage Report — Day 80

A risk-based vulnerability prioritisation exercise considering more than severity alone, including asset importance, exposure, exploitation, applicability, and remediation considerations. It demonstrates contextual risk assessment and defensible prioritisation.

## 10. Cybersecurity Foundations Portfolio — Day 90

This consolidated portfolio brings together the nine foundational artifacts from the 90-day journey. It demonstrates progression across security concepts, investigation, defensive planning, attack analysis, and risk-based decision-making.

# PERSONAL STATEMENT

## Ninety Days of Learning, Investigation, and Growth

Ninety days ago, I set out to build a proper foundation in cybersecurity. I wanted to understand how attacks happen, how systems can be protected, and how security professionals investigate problems rather than simply learning definitions and tools.

When I started, many concepts felt separate. Today, I can see how networking, Linux, threat analysis, defensive controls, and risk management connect.

Two things particularly changed how I think about security. First, I learned that a vulnerability's severity does not automatically determine how urgently it should be fixed. Exposure, business impact, active exploitation, and the environment matter just as much. Second, studying the Cyber Kill Chain helped me see an attack as a sequence of connected stages rather than a single suspicious event. It also made me think more carefully about where defenders can detect and interrupt that sequence.

I am particularly proud of the Linux Forensics Investigation and Maya Network Defence Plan. They challenged me to look beyond individual security concepts: to correlate different sources of system evidence in an investigation and to turn identified network weaknesses into practical defensive recommendations. The Vulnerability Triage exercise also helped me understand why security decisions need clear reasoning, not just severity labels.

This journey has made me increasingly interested in defensive cybersecurity, especially Security Operations and incident investigation. I enjoy examining evidence, understanding suspicious activity, identifying what matters, and communicating findings clearly.

I know there is still a great deal to learn, but I now have a stronger foundation and a clearer direction.

My next step is to apply for entry-level defensive security opportunities, including SOC roles, while continuing to improve through hands-on labs, investigations, and personal projects.

I see these ninety days not as the end of my learning, but as the point from which I can build more confidently and contribute meaningfully to a security team.
