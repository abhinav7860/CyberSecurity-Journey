# What is Phishing?

Phishing is a social engineering attack where an attacker pretends to be a trusted person or organization to trick victims into revealing sensitive information.

The attacker may try to steal:

- Usernames
- Passwords
- Credit card details
- Banking information
- Two-Factor Authentication codes

Instead of hacking computers, phishing hacks **human trust**.

---

# Why Phishing Still Works

Most people think phishing emails are easy to spot because they contain poor grammar or obvious mistakes.

That was true years ago.

Today, phishing emails are:

- Professionally written
- Personalized
- Well formatted
- Free of spelling mistakes
- Nearly identical to legitimate emails

Modern phishing relies on psychology rather than poor technical security.

According to multiple cybersecurity reports, **70–90% of successful cyberattacks begin with phishing or some form of social engineering.**

---

# Anatomy of a Modern Phishing Email

A phishing email is designed to look legitimate while hiding small details that reveal it is fake.

Example:

```
From:
Google Security
security-noreply@accounts-google.co
```

Subject:

```
Suspicious Sign-in Blocked
```

Message:

```
We blocked a login attempt from Romania.

Please secure your account within 24 hours.
```

A large button:

```
Secure My Account
```

At first glance everything looks real.

---

# What Makes a Phishing Email Convincing

Attackers usually get several things right.

## Legitimate Sender Name

Instead of displaying:

```
John Smith
```

They display:

```
Google Security
```

Most people only read the sender name.

---

## Personalized Greeting

Instead of saying:

```
Dear Customer
```

The email uses:

```
Hi Abhinav
```

Personalization makes the email feel authentic.

---

## Realistic Details

Examples include:

- Actual city names
- Login timestamps
- IP addresses
- Device information

These details increase trust.

---

## Professional Formatting

Modern phishing emails often look identical to legitimate emails.

They include:

- Company logos
- Correct fonts
- Brand colors
- Official signatures

---

## Natural Urgency

Instead of shouting:

```
CLICK NOW!!!
```

They say:

```
Please verify your account within 24 hours.
```

The urgency feels believable.

---

# How to Identify a Phishing Email

Even convincing phishing emails usually contain small clues.

## Check the Sender Domain

Example:

```
security@accounts-google.co
```

Looks real.

Actual Google domain:

```
google.com
```

Notice:

```
.co

≠

.com
```

Always inspect the domain after the **@** symbol.

---

## Hover Over Links

Never click immediately.

Hover your mouse over the button.

Example:

Visible button:

```
Secure My Account
```

Actual destination:

```
accounts-google.co/login
```

The displayed text and actual URL are different.

This is one of the biggest phishing indicators.

---

## Look for Unnecessary Urgency

Examples:

- Verify within 24 hours
- Account will be suspended
- Immediate action required
- Payment failed

Attackers want victims to react emotionally rather than think logically.

---

# Three Psychological Tricks Used by Attackers

Today's lesson explained that almost every phishing attack relies on one or more of three emotions.

---

## 1. Urgency

Examples:

- Your account will expire.
- Verify immediately.
- Payment failed.

Urgency causes people to act before thinking.

---

## 2. Authority

Examples:

- CEO
- Bank
- Google
- IT Department
- Government

People naturally obey authority figures.

Attackers abuse this instinct.

---

## 3. Curiosity

Examples:

- Someone shared a document.
- You received a package.
- Payment confirmation.
- New voicemail.

Curiosity encourages victims to click links.

---

# Decision Fatigue

One interesting concept I learned today is **Decision Fatigue**.

After making hundreds of decisions during the day, people become mentally tired.

When this happens they:

- Think less
- Click faster
- Verify less

This is one reason phishing campaigns are often sent:

- Friday afternoons
- End of workdays
- During busy periods

---

# Five Habits That Prevent Phishing

Instead of trying to identify every fake email, today's lesson recommended building habits.

---

## 1. Hover Before Clicking

Always inspect links before clicking.

This simple habit catches many phishing attempts.

---

## 2. Never Login Through Email Links

If an email asks me to log in:

Wrong approach:

```
Email

↓

Click Link

↓

Login
```

Correct approach:

```
Close Email

↓

Open Browser

↓

Visit Official Website

↓

Login Normally
```

If the notification is genuine, it will appear after logging in directly.

---

## 3. Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Even if an attacker steals my password,

they still need:

- Authenticator App
- Security Key
- Mobile Device

Without the second factor,

the stolen password becomes much less useful.

---

## 4. Treat Urgency as Suspicious

If an email tries to rush me,

I should slow down.

Urgency is one of the strongest phishing indicators.

---

## 5. Verify Through Another Channel

Example:

Email says:

```
Boss requests money transfer.
```

Instead of replying,

I should:

- Call them
- Message on Teams
- Send a WhatsApp message

Never trust only one communication channel.

---

# AI and Modern Phishing

Today's phishing attacks are becoming much more dangerous because of Artificial Intelligence.

Attackers can now generate:

- Perfect grammar
- Personalized emails
- Fake voices
- Deepfake videos

This makes phishing much harder to detect.

However,

the five habits above still work regardless of whether the attack was created by a human or AI.

---

# Common Types of Phishing

## Email Phishing

The most common type.

Delivered through email.

---

## Spear Phishing

Targets one specific person.

Usually personalized.

---

## Whaling

Targets high-ranking executives.

Examples:

- CEO
- CFO
- Managers

---

## Smishing

Phishing through SMS messages.

---

## Vishing

Phishing using phone calls.

Attackers pretend to be:

- Bank employees
- Technical support
- Government officials

---

## Clone Phishing

A legitimate email is copied.

Only the malicious link or attachment is replaced.

---

# Real-World Examples

Some famous phishing attacks mentioned today include:

- John Podesta Gmail Phishing (2016)
- Twitter Account Hijack (2020)
- AI-generated deepfake finance scams (2024)

These incidents demonstrate that phishing can impact individuals, businesses, and even governments.
