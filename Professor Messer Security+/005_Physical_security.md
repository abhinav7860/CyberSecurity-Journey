# Why Physical Security Matters

Cybersecurity isn't just about protecting computers and networks.

If an attacker can physically enter a building or access a server room, they may bypass many technical security controls.

Physical security helps protect:

- Buildings
- Employees
- Servers
- Networking equipment
- Sensitive information

---

# 1. Barricades / Bollards

Barricades and bollards are physical barriers used to control access.

Their main purpose is to stop vehicles or unauthorized people from reaching protected areas.

### Uses

- Prevent unauthorized vehicle access
- Guide people through a specific entrance
- Protect buildings from vehicle attacks
- Improve pedestrian safety

### Examples

- Concrete bollards
- Steel barriers
- Security gates

### Easy to Remember

> Bollards stop vehicles, not people.

---

# 2. Access Control Vestibules (Mantraps)

An Access Control Vestibule, also called a **Mantrap**, is a secure entry area with two or more doors.

Only one door can be opened at a time.

A person must pass authentication before entering the next area.

### How it Works

```
Outside
    │
Door 1
    │
Verification
    │
Door 2
    │
Secure Area
```

### Benefits

- Prevents tailgating
- Controls the number of people entering
- Protects restricted areas
- Improves physical access control

### Common Locations

- Data Centers
- Banks
- Research Labs
- Government Buildings

### Easy to Remember

> One door opens, the other stays locked.

---

# 3. Fencing

Fences create a secure perimeter around a facility.

They make it harder for intruders to enter restricted areas.

### Types

- Chain-link fence
- Metal fence
- Concrete wall
- Razor wire fencing

### Good Security Fences

- Strong and difficult to cut
- Tall enough to prevent climbing
- Clearly define the property boundary

### Easy to Remember

> Fence = First physical layer of defense.

---

# 4. Video Surveillance (CCTV)

CCTV (Closed-Circuit Television) continuously monitors an area.

Modern cameras can do much more than simply record video.

### Features

- Motion detection
- Object detection
- Facial recognition
- License plate recognition
- Remote monitoring

### Benefits

- Deters attackers
- Records incidents
- Helps during investigations
- Monitors large areas

### Easy to Remember

> CCTV watches everything, even when no guard is present.

---

# 5. Security Guards

Security guards provide physical protection for a facility.

Their responsibilities include:

- Monitoring entrances
- Verifying employee identity
- Checking visitor passes
- Responding to incidents
- Patrolling the building

### Two-Person Integrity (TPI)

Some sensitive areas require two authorized people to enter together.

This reduces the risk of insider attacks.

### Example

A server room containing critical infrastructure may require two administrators before access is granted.

---

# 6. Access Badges

Access badges identify employees and control building access.

Most badges contain:

- Employee photo
- Name
- ID number
- RFID or Smart Card chip

Many organizations log badge activity electronically.

This creates an audit trail showing:

- Entry time
- Exit time
- Accessed locations

### Easy to Remember

> Badge = Identity + Access.

---

# 7. Security Lighting

Proper lighting improves physical security.

Attackers usually prefer dark areas where they are less visible.

Good lighting helps:

- Security guards
- CCTV cameras
- Employees
- Visitors

### Good Lighting Design

- Reduce shadows
- Avoid glare
- Cover parking lots
- Illuminate entrances and exits

### Easy to Remember

> More light = Better visibility = Better security.

---

# 8. Physical Security Sensors

Sensors automatically detect movement or unusual activity.

---

## Infrared (IR)

Detects infrared radiation (heat).

Commonly used in:

- Motion detectors
- Alarm systems

---

## Pressure Sensors

Detect changes in pressure.

Examples:

- Pressure-sensitive floor mats
- Window sensors

---

## Microwave Sensors

Use microwave signals to detect movement across large areas.

Commonly used for:

- Warehouses
- Outdoor security

---

## Ultrasonic Sensors

Transmit ultrasonic sound waves and detect their reflections.

Used for:

- Motion detection
- Collision detection
- Automatic doors

# Real-Life Example

Imagine entering a company's headquarters.

1. A fence surrounds the property.
2. Bollards stop unauthorized vehicles.
3. Security cameras monitor the entrance.
4. A security guard checks your identity.
5. You scan your access badge.
6. You enter through a mantrap.
7. Motion sensors monitor restricted areas.
8. Bright lighting helps cameras capture clear footage.

All of these controls work together to protect the building.

# Exam Tips 📝

✅ Bollards mainly stop **vehicles**.

✅ Mantraps help prevent **tailgating**.

✅ CCTV is both a **deterrent** and a **detective** control.

✅ Access badges provide **identification** and **access control**.

✅ Good lighting improves both **human visibility** and **camera performance**.

✅ Infrared sensors detect **heat**, while ultrasonic sensors use **sound waves**.
