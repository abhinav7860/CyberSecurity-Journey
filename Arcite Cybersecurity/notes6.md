# What are Security Standards?

Security standards are officially documented guidelines that help organizations protect their systems, networks, and sensitive information.

They act as a roadmap for implementing cybersecurity best practices.

Instead of every company creating its own security rules, they follow internationally accepted standards.

---

# Why Security Standards are Important

Security standards help organizations:

- Protect sensitive data
- Reduce cybersecurity risks
- Meet legal and regulatory requirements
- Improve customer trust
- Prepare for audits
- Build a strong security posture

Without standards, every company would implement security differently, making protection inconsistent.

# ISO/IEC 27001

## What is ISO 27001?

ISO/IEC 27001 is an internationally recognized cybersecurity standard for creating and maintaining an Information Security Management System (ISMS).

It was developed by:

- ISO (International Organization for Standardization)
- IEC (International Electrotechnical Commission)

It focuses on protecting information through proper risk management.

---

# Information Security Management System (ISMS)

An ISMS is a structured framework of:

- Policies
- Procedures
- Security Controls
- Documentation

Its purpose is to protect information by maintaining the CIA Triad.

### CIA Triad

### Confidentiality

Only authorized users should access information.

Example:

Only HR should access employee salary records.

---

### Integrity

Information should remain accurate and unmodified.

Example:

Student grades should not be altered without authorization.

---

### Availability

Information should always be accessible when required.

Example:

A company's website should remain available to customers.

---

# Annex A Security Controls

ISO 27001 provides **114 security controls** under Annex A.

These controls help organizations secure their infrastructure.

They are divided into three main categories.

---

## Technical Controls

Examples:

- Firewalls
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
- Encryption
- Access Control
- Antivirus

---

## Organizational Controls

Examples:

- Security Awareness Training
- Security Policies
- Employee Responsibilities
- Incident Reporting

---

## Physical Controls

Examples:

- CCTV Cameras
- Biometric Access
- Door Locks
- Security Guards

---

# Important Control Categories

Some major Annex A categories include:

- Access Control
- Asset Management
- Cryptography
- Supplier Relationships
- Incident Management

These controls help organizations manage different aspects of cybersecurity.

---

# ISO 27001 Clauses (4–10)

ISO certification follows seven important management clauses.

| Clause | Purpose |
|---------|----------|
| Clause 4 | Understand the organization and its environment |
| Clause 5 | Leadership commitment |
| Clause 6 | Risk assessment and planning |
| Clause 7 | Resources, awareness, and communication |
| Clause 8 | Implement security controls |
| Clause 9 | Monitor and evaluate effectiveness |
| Clause 10 | Continuous improvement |

# NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)

## What is NIST?

NIST stands for:

**National Institute of Standards and Technology**

It is a U.S. government agency that publishes free cybersecurity standards and guidelines.

Unlike ISO certification, NIST is generally voluntary and freely available.

---

# NIST Cybersecurity Framework

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework helps organizations identify, manage, and reduce cybersecurity risks.

It aligns technical security with business objectives.

It is widely used by:

- Government agencies
- Critical infrastructure
- Schools
- Banks
- Enterprises

---

# Five Core Functions of NIST CSF

The framework follows five continuous stages.

---

## 1. Identify

Understand what needs protection.

Includes:

- Assets
- Systems
- Data
- Users
- Risk Assessments

Example:

Creating an inventory of company servers and databases.

---

## 2. Protect

Implement safeguards to reduce risk.

Examples:

- Access Control
- Encryption
- Employee Training
- Secure Configurations

---

## 3. Detect

Identify threats as early as possible.

Examples:

- Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS)
- Security Logs
- Alerts
- Monitoring Tools

Example:

Snort detecting suspicious network traffic.

---

## 4. Respond

Take action after detecting an incident.

Includes:

- Incident Response Plans
- Containment
- Communication
- Investigation

Goal:

Reduce damage quickly.

---

## 5. Recover

Restore systems after an attack.

Includes:

- Backups
- Disaster Recovery
- Business Continuity
- System Restoration

Goal:

Return operations to normal as quickly as possible.

---

# Real-Life Example

Imagine a company hosting customer information.

### Identify

Inventory:

- Web Server
- Customer Database
- Sensitive Information

---

### Protect

Implement:

- Firewall
- Encryption
- Employee Training

---

### Detect

Monitor traffic using IDS tools like Snort.

---

### Respond

Follow an Incident Response Plan if suspicious activity is detected.

---

### Recover

Restore systems using nightly backups.

---

# NIST Summary

The NIST framework follows a simple lifecycle:

```
Identify
     ↓
Protect
     ↓
Detect
     ↓
Respond
     ↓
Recover
```

It provides practical guidance for building strong cybersecurity programs.

# GDPR

## What is GDPR?

GDPR stands for:

**General Data Protection Regulation**

It is a European Union law created to protect the privacy and personal information of individuals.

Although it is an EU law, it affects organizations worldwide if they process the data of EU citizens.

---

# Why GDPR Was Created

GDPR was introduced to:

- Protect personal information
- Prevent misuse of user data
- Improve transparency
- Hold organizations accountable

---

# What is Personal Data?

Personal data includes any information that identifies an individual.

Examples include:

- Name
- Email Address
- Phone Number
- IP Address
- Location Data
- Biometric Information

---

# Key Principles of GDPR

## Data Minimization

Only collect the information that is absolutely necessary.

Example:

A newsletter only needs an email address, not a home address.

---

## Lawful Processing

Organizations must have a legal reason to collect user data.

Examples include:

- Consent
- Contract
- Legal obligation

---

## Informed Consent

Users must clearly understand:

- What data is collected
- Why it is collected
- How it will be used

Consent should always be voluntary.

---

## Right to Access and Erasure

Users have the right to:

- View their stored data
- Correct inaccurate information
- Request deletion of their data

This is known as the **Right to be Forgotten**.

---

## Breach Notification

If a data breach occurs,

organizations must notify regulators and affected users within **72 hours**.

---

# Real-World Example

WhatsApp updated its privacy policy because of GDPR.

Users were informed about:

- Data collection
- Data sharing
- Privacy settings

EU users received additional privacy controls.

---

# Companies Penalized Under GDPR

Several major companies have received heavy fines, including:

- Google
- Meta
- Amazon

These fines demonstrate that organizations must take user privacy seriously.
